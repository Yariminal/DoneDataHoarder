## Proposal-only naming and organization audit

The reproducible command is `python scripts/audit_prior_proposals.py --validation-root D:\Test --output-parent D:\Test`. It selects the latest six earlier validation databases, backs each up with SQLite's backup API, hashes each source database before and after, and rebuilds eligible pending rename and move proposals only in the copies. The audit forces the copied session language to `leave_as_is`, so it does not call a translation model. It invokes no file-operation executor. Every source database hash matched before and after. The private audit JSON retains per-corpus counts, original language preference, suppression reasons, and the two candidate names with each file's own evidence.

The 1,251 preserved files are a safety classification, **not** 1,251 newly organized files. The four project-heavy corpora contributed 96, 34, 525, and 596 preserved files. The other two corpora contributed 16 and 54 independent files. Seven independent move proposals remain reviewable, while 46 independent files still need review. All 46 are protected linked resources in the copied audit (11 in one corpus, 35 in the other); none fell into unsupported/skipped, unverified-analysis, or other categories after that safety-first classification. Protection explains why they stayed put, not whether their existing folders are ideal. The 17 files retained in existing folders met a structural heuristic; their subject/date placement has not been verified. These prior corpora were project-heavy and cannot measure standalone-file organization quality for a large mixed library.

Only two pending rename suggestions survived on the copies. Both were text-backed office documents in one earlier corpus: a presentation (759 extracted characters) and a guidance document (1,517 extracted characters). Root independently checked the original PPTX/DOCX XML and found both content descriptions supported (2/2 reviewed suggestions, not corpus accuracy). The presentation's proposed date matches Office modified metadata. The guidance document's proposed date comes from filesystem modified time and differs from its Office created/modified metadata. Review now exposes that filename-date provenance, so the date is not presented as a verified document event. The exact names, topics, dates, and evidence are in the private audit. A third candidate would have lost a dotted numeric source identifier; the final guard suppressed it as `source_numeric_identifier_lost`. Aggregate suppression reasons include project-subtree preservation, numbered-frame preservation, protected resources, an unsupported relation-name transfer, an unsupported ordinal, and that dotted numeric identifier. These are counts of proposed operations suppressed, not independent ground-truth labels of final naming quality.

Three separate literal-text fixtures exercise useful subject naming for pump inspection, greenhouse irrigation, and privacy meeting minutes. The source text itself contains each proposed subject. They are authored regression controls, not an unseen holdout or evidence for a population useful-name rate. The earlier private review found one ambiguous render-shape description and one model-invented technical floor interpretation; model confidence or its own prose is not treated as independent evidence. No broad rename precision, usefulness, or recall percentage is claimed.

The combined-run proposal audit at `9c2861a` reviewed every proposed filesystem rename and move before execution. Root read the original Office XML for all five rename candidates: four broad subject descriptions had source support and one misleadingly treated a planning document as a photo. Only one was selected for the rename/undo proof; three others were deferred because the change offered limited value or relied on an uncertain date. A matching metadata date was not treated as proof of the underlying event date, and one deferred suggestion used a filesystem date that differed from Office modified metadata. All seven move proposals would split reviewer-identified named collections, so none was approved. These counts are a complete review of the combined run's five renames and seven moves, not a population accuracy or usefulness rate. Keeping project files in place avoided those moves; it did not newly organize them or establish that their current folders are ideal.

## Duplicate and sequence review

The private frozen-pair audit found that visually similar files can have changed text or geometry, including an identical perceptual hash with visibly changed screen text. It did not authorize disposal. Numbered families now produce at most stratified comparison samples; unsampled frames remain untouched and are not classified as duplicates. Non-exact proposals stay individual-review only. Review labels call them similarity comparisons, and the UI shows a selected keeper alongside a bounded closest observed direct perceptual-hash peer when available. For groups larger than the peer scan limit, the comparison is labeled sampled. Candidate-generation caps now report `bounded_incomplete`; a durable review warning keeps an unknown deferred-pair count unknown. Absence of a cap does not certify complete similarity recall.

The frozen-pair labels form a candidate-selected set. The earlier 31-pair known non-exact sample had only 3 same-image derivatives and 28 distinct pairs (3/31 candidate-edge precision). Eleven adjacent/nearby frame comparisons were all distinct. On a copied-database refresh, one numbered-frame corpus fell from 476 to 5 non-exact review edges: three sampled sequence comparisons and two content-verified semantic comparisons; all five residual edges were visually distinct. A separate blind selected pack of 16 actual new keeper-relative edges contained 15 visually distinct pairs and one same-imagery alpha/background role variant; no interchangeable duplicate was established. Neither set establishes collection-wide recall or safe-disposal precision. Final candidate burden and precision must be measured after the revised algorithm on the same labeled fixture, including direct keeper-relative comparisons and the number of deferred opportunities.

The combined `9c2861a` run produced 159 duplicate proposals: 94 exact-copy edges and 65 non-exact review comparisons. Root independently matched the 94 exact edges to 57 groups, 151 members, and direct ZIP-stream hashes, with no missing or false exact group in that collection. A new blind stratified sample of 16 of the 65 non-exact keeper-relative comparisons was labeled before revealing per-pair score or type: 10 distinct, 5 derivative or role variants, 1 uncertain same-scene pair, and 0 established interchangeable copies. No non-exact proposal was approved for disposal. The 16/65 sample is not a random population estimate and says nothing about retrieval recall.

The linked-asset audit used earlier indexed corpora and kept file paths private. It found 24/34 protected in a photo/CAD corpus, 11/16 in a web/design-links corpus, 532/596 in a CAD/font/eTransmit corpus, and 96/96 in a `.fbm` media-bundle corpus. Category counts overlap and are not a proof that every external reference resolves. The whole `.fbm` bundle was preserved; its 96 unchanged files are safety coverage, not useful file moves. The implementation has no reference rewrite or atomic whole-bundle relocation. Real HTML rendering and Adobe/3D consuming-app opening remain open. A separate physical Windows junction proof placed an actual `lstat`-confirmed junction inside an isolated collection, pointing to a sibling external target. Walk, scan, and preflight included only the two local files with no scan errors; dependency indexing made zero external `Path.open` reads and zero external `os.scandir` traversals, and protected the page and junction. Preflight, scan, and the walker also rejected the junction as a selected root. This proves the tested traversal boundary, not how legacy previously indexed junction paths would behave.

The independent reviewer found the current non-exact retrieval still has low utility in the selected blind pack: 15/16 keeper-relative comparisons were distinct and the remaining one was a role variant, not an interchangeable copy. Broad protection for opaque web/design bundles may also retain unrelated assets; tighter boundaries require credible reference evidence and consuming-app checks. Large-group keeper-switch latency has not been profiled. These are priorities for review quality and scale, not claims that protected or unreviewed files are safe to alter.
